QV Observatory

Words travel. A word like resilience can start in an EU strategy paper, move into parliament speeches, reach the news, and end up in exhibition texts — or go the other way. This site reads thousands of European documents and counts how often each word is used, month by month.

How does this work?
  1. We collect texts from four worlds: EU policy (European Commission documents), politics (European Parliament debates), the news (European newspapers and broadcasters) and art (art magazines and announcements).
  2. We look for a handful of words that have become fashionable in Europe — resilience, community, sustainability, care, inclusion, decolonial, polycrisis — in seven languages (for example resilience, résilience, resiliência, veerkracht).
  3. We count the share of documents that use each word, every month. 30% means 3 in 10 documents used it at least once.
  4. We compare the four worlds: where did the word rise first, and how many months did the others take to follow?
  5. We look at the company it keeps: the words that appear right next to it, year by year. When those neighbours change, the meaning of the word is changing.

The project is the machine-reading half of QV's book Os Últimos Textos Humanos: the machine reads Europe, and QV writes back. More at the bottom of the page.
